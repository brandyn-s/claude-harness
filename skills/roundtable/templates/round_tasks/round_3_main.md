## Round 3 — MAIN (defend/concede with falsifiers)

You now have access to the OTHER agents' Round 2 outputs (their critiques of your Round 1 work) AND your own Round 3 pre-registration.

### Your task

For each Round 2 critique that targeted YOUR Round 1 work:
- Quote the critique (1-2 sentences)
- Mark response: **DEFEND**, **CONCEDE**, or **PARTIAL**
- For DEFEND: provide a concrete rebuttal — cite source, identify what the critic missed
- For CONCEDE: state what you got wrong and what you now believe instead, with corrected confidence
- For PARTIAL: split the critique into valid and invalid parts
- **Falsifier**: state ONE specific observation that would change your verdict (DEFEND→CONCEDE or vice versa). If you can't name one, you're not actually calibrated.

### Compare against your pre-registration

After your defend/concede responses, add a section comparing your actual Round 3 stance to your pre-reg:

- Where pre-reg predicted DEFEND but you ended up CONCEDING — what changed your mind?
- Where pre-reg predicted CONCEDE but you ended up DEFENDING — what argument was weaker than expected?
- Did the actual critiques target the findings you predicted, or different ones?

### Cross-fire commentary (optional, brief)

If you can see clearly that Agent A's defense against Agent B is wrong (or vice versa), say so. Roundtable benefits from third-party adjudication.

### What to avoid

- **Do not concede out of politeness.** If the critique mischaracterizes your claim, defend with evidence.
- **Concession requires citing specific peer evidence that flipped you.** State which sentence or claim from which agent moved you. "On reflection..." or "I see their point..." without a specific citation is rubber-stamp agreement and gets flagged. If you cannot point to the specific evidence, you are conceding to social pressure, not argument.
- **Your falsifier must reference what would re-flip you.** After conceding, name the observation that would convince you the original position was right. If you cannot name one, your concession is unprincipled and you should defend instead.
- **Do not defend by restating your original claim louder.** A defense must produce evidence the critic did not have or did not weigh correctly.
- **Do not retreat to vagueness.** If you concede, be specific about what you now believe.

```

**Response**: [DEFEND | CONCEDE | PARTIAL]
[reasoning]
**Falsifier**: [specific observation]

[...repeat for all critiques against you...]

## Cross-fire commentary (optional, brief)
```

Length: 800-1500 words. Direct prose. Calibration over hedge.
